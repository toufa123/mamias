MAMIAS — Marine Mediterranean Invasive Alien Species — is the regional information platform that gathers, harmonises and shares what we know about non-indigenous and invasive marine life in the Mediterranean Sea. It serves science, policy and the public, under the mandate of the Barcelona Convention.

01 — What is MAMIAS

A regional brain
for a shared sea.

The Mediterranean is one of the most biologically invaded seas on Earth. Information about its newcomers — when they arrived, how, where they spread and what they affect — has long been scattered across journals, institutional databases and field notebooks. MAMIAS exists to bring that knowledge together into a single, continuously updated, openly accessible reference.

It is operated by SPA/RAC — the Specially Protected Areas Regional Activity Centre of the UN Environment Programme / Mediterranean Action Plan — and supports the obligations of the Barcelona Convention's SPA/BD Protocol on non-indigenous species.

Database

A basin-wide registry of alien species with taxonomy, biology, distribution and impact.

Each species record carries year of first introduction, first record per country, primary and secondary pathways, establishment status, and documented impacts on biodiversity, human health and ecosystem services.

02 — Pathways of introduction

How they
get in.

New alien species reach the Mediterranean through a handful of well-identified routes. Knowing the pathway is the first step toward prevention — it tells us which sector, which corridor, which behaviour to address. MAMIAS records the pathway for every species, together with a confidence level.

01

Shipping

Ballast waters and hull fouling — the historic and still dominant vector.

02

Corridors

Lessepsian migration via the Suez Canal — a continuous influx of Indo-Pacific species.

03

Maritime transport & waterways

Channels, ports and inland connections that reshape natural barriers.

04

Aquaculture

Intentional or accidental release of farmed species and their associated organisms.

05

Live trade

Aquarium trade and fishing bait — small flows that have outsized consequences.

06

Fishing & exhibits

Bycatch transfers, escapes from public aquariums and similar incidental routes.

07

Climate change

A warming basin lowers the threshold for tropical species to settle and spread.

∞

Secondary spread

Once established, species move along Mediterranean currents and human activity — the loop continues.

03 — A basin, twenty-two parties

A network that mirrors the sea.

Twenty-one Mediterranean countries and the European Union are Contracting Parties to the Barcelona Convention. Each one contributes national reports, baseline studies and annual updates — turning local fieldwork into a regional picture.

  • ● Each Party operates through a National Focal Point for SPAs.
  • ● SPA/RAC coordinates reporting and harmonises the data model.
  • ● MAMIAS is linked to EASIN and other international networks.

04 — Mandate

The frame
that holds us.

MAMIAS is not a stand-alone initiative. It sits inside a layered legal and scientific architecture that gives it both purpose and obligation.

1976 / 1995
Barcelona Convention & SPA/BD Protocol
+

Article 13 of the SPA/BD Protocol invites Parties to regulate intentional and non-intentional introduction of non-indigenous species, prohibit those with harmful impacts, and endeavour to eradicate established invasive species.

1992 — Rio
Convention on Biological Diversity, Art. 8(h)
+

Each Party is to prevent the introduction of, control or eradicate those alien species which threaten ecosystems, habitats or species. Operationalised by Aichi Target 9.

2014 — EU
Regulation (EU) 1143/2014 on Invasive Alien Species
+

Establishes prevention, early warning, rapid response and management obligations for species of EU concern — complemented by the Marine Strategy Framework Directive (2008/56/EC) and the EASIN network.

EcAp
Ecosystem Approach & Common Indicator 6
+

MAMIAS feeds the Integrated Monitoring and Assessment Programme of the Barcelona Convention — particularly the trends-in-non-indigenous-species indicator used to assess Good Environmental Status.

IMO
Ballast Water Management Convention
+

National legislation across the basin is encouraged to mirror the IMO Convention on ballast water, together with codes of practice from ICES, IUCN and FAO.

05 — Implementation timetable

A five-year
rolling plan.

Drawn from the Annex of the Action Plan on Species Introductions and Invasive Species — the steps that brought MAMIAS into being and now keep it alive.

  1. 2016

    Launch of MAMIAS

    SPA/RAC initiates the platform; Contracting Parties prepare national reports and set up coordination mechanisms.

  2. 2017

    Baseline studies & monitoring

    Reporting forms, online search tools, directories of specialists, and the first national legislation programmes go live.

  3. 2018

    Mapping & risk assessment

    Online mapping is released; regional training sessions are organised; risk-assessment techniques are deployed nationally.

  4. 2019

    Early warning & National Plans

    The notification system is integrated; Parties adopt National Plans; MAMIAS connects with EASIN and other international systems.

  5. 2020 →

    Statistics, indicators & outreach

    Statistical tools for EcAp reporting; public education material; a triennial symposium gathering Mediterranean specialists.
