The data explorer (**Explore MAMIAS › Data**, /pages/data) lists every
non-indigenous species (NIS) in the MAMIAS catalogue, one row per species, with
its first record in the Mediterranean. Search it, narrow it with filters, and
open any species for its full record. Anyone can use it; no account is needed.

## The page at a glance

![Filters above the species table](/images/docs/data/01-page.png)

The page has two blocks:

1. **Filters**, which narrow the table.
2. **Non-indigenous species**, the table itself.

Click the title bar of either block to fold it away, and again to open it.
**Guide** at the top right opens this page.

## Filters

Pick the values you want, then click **Apply filters**: the table changes only
then, so you can set several filters at once. **Reset**, in the filters' title
bar, clears them all. Within a filter you can pick several values; a species is
kept when it matches any of them. Across filters, it has to match all of them.

| Filter | Keeps the species… |
| --- | --- |
| 1st Year of Introduction | first recorded in the Mediterranean between the two years. Drag either handle; with both at the ends, species whose year is unknown are kept too |
| 1st Country of Introduction | first recorded in one of the countries you pick |
| NIS Status | with one of the statuses you pick: NIS, cryptogenic, questionable, range expansion, data deficient |
| Establishment Status | established, casual, invasive and so on, for the Mediterranean as a whole |
| EcAp Subregion | recorded in one of the EcAp subregions you pick |
| Present in Country | recorded in one of the countries you pick, whether or not it was first recorded there |
| CBD Pathway Category, Subcategory | introduced by the pathway you pick, in the CBD classification; the subcategories follow the category you picked |
| Pathway Type, Uncertainty | by the type of pathway, or how sure the experts are of it |

![Filters applied: the active filters above the table](/images/docs/data/02-filtered.png)

Once applied, each filter shows as a chip above the table, **Active filters**.
Click the **×** on a chip to drop that filter alone.

## The species table

| Column | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| NIS Scientific Name | The species, as accepted by WoRMS, in italics, with its authority. "Recorded as …" underneath when it was first published under another name |
| 1st Year of Introduction | Year of its first record in the Mediterranean |
| 1st Country of Introduction | Country of that first record |
| NIS Status | NIS, cryptogenic, questionable, range expansion or data deficient |
| Establishment Status | Established, invasive, casual, and so on |

- **Search** finds species by name, or by family (try *Gobiidae*), as you type.
- Click a column title with arrows to sort by it, again to reverse the order.
  The table starts sorted by name.
- Under the table, choose how many species to show per page, and move between
  pages.
- Click a species, or **View data** at the end of its row, to open its page.

## A species page

![A species page](/images/docs/data/03-species.png)

Everything MAMIAS holds on one species, in three parts:

- **The species**, on three tabs. The first, titled with its name and
  authority, gives its classification (kingdom to genus), its Aphia ID (a link
  to WoRMS), EASIN ID, LSID (click to copy), rank, environment and statuses.
  **Synonyms** lists the other names WoRMS knows for it, **References** the
  publications behind its record; both tabs show how many there are.
- **Introduction event.** Its first Mediterranean record: year, country, NIS
  and establishment status, and the reference that documents it. Below, tabs
  for the **Sub-regions** and **Countries** it has reached (with their own
  status and first record) and its **Pathways** of introduction.
- **Occurrences.** A map of every approved sighting; click a pin for its date,
  coordinates, depth, abundance and habitats.

Click the title bar of Introduction event or Occurrences to fold it. The
breadcrumb at the top leads back to the data explorer.

To see where the species are rather than list them, open the map
(**Explore MAMIAS › MAP**).
